<p class="lead">The most stressful part of running a small shop isn't making things. It's deciding what to charge for them. Here's the framework I use so I don't have to relitigate the question every single time.</p>

For the first year I priced everything by vibe: I'd look at a finished print, guess a number that didn't feel greedy, and undercharge by roughly half. Vibes are not a pricing strategy. What follows is boring, repeatable, and has kept the shop from quietly losing money.

## <span class="idx">01</span>Start from cost, not from the competition

Looking at what other people charge tells you what the market tolerates, not what your object costs you to exist. I start from the ground up: materials, plus my time, plus the overhead everyone forgets — packaging, listing fees, the shipping supplies, the failed prototypes.

> If your price doesn't cover the mistakes, the mistakes come out of your pay.

## <span class="idx">02</span>The formula I actually use

It's deliberately simple. I'd rather use a rough formula every time than a perfect one never.

<table class="bom">
<tr><th>Component</th><th>How I estimate it</th><th>Example</th></tr>
<tr><td>Materials</td><td>Real cost per unit, including waste</td><td>$6</td></tr>
<tr><td>Labor</td><td>Honest minutes × a real hourly rate</td><td>$14</td></tr>
<tr><td>Overhead</td><td>Flat ~20% of the above</td><td>$4</td></tr>
<tr><td>Base cost</td><td>Sum of the three</td><td>$24</td></tr>
<tr><td>Retail</td><td>Base × 2 (covers fees + margin)</td><td>$48</td></tr>
</table>

## <span class="idx">03</span>Why the ×2 isn't greedy

That multiplier feels aggressive until you subtract everything it absorbs: platform fees, payment processing, the discount codes, the returns, and the fact that you can't work every hour of the day. The margin isn't profit — it's the buffer that lets the shop survive a slow month.

## <span class="idx">04</span>When to break your own rule

- Loss-leaders — a cheap sticker or card that gets someone in the door.
- One-of-a-kind pieces, where scarcity does the pricing for you.
- Wholesale, which needs its own math entirely, not a discount hack.

The point of a framework isn't to obey it blindly. It's to know the real number first, so that when you choose to charge less, it's a decision — not an accident.
